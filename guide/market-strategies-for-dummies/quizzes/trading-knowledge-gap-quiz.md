# How to use this quiz

**Purpose.** Find what you understand vs. what you only recognize by name. Gaps matter more than a high score.

**Rules.**

1. No charts open, no Google, no notes on the first pass.
2. Write answers on paper or mark choices clearly.
3. If you are unsure, mark **?** — that is useful data.
4. Score with the answer key at the end. Note which **sections** you miss, not only total percent.

**Suggested scoring per section**

| Result | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| 80%+ | Solid working knowledge |
| 50–79% | Partial — review that topic soon |
| Under 50% | Priority gap — study before sizing up risk |

**Time.** About 35–50 minutes.

## Part D — model answers

**D1. Double top:** two peaks near same high; bearish reversal bias after neckline break; invalidation if price reclaims and makes a clean new high / failed breakdown.

**D2. Double bottom:** two lows near same level; bullish reversal bias after neckline break; invalidation on clean new low / failed breakout back below lows.

**D3. H&S:** left shoulder–head–right shoulder; bearish after neckline break; invalidation if right side fails and price reclaims strongly above neckline/head area.

**D4. Ascending triangle:** rising lows + flat highs; often bullish continuation bias; invalidation on breakdown below rising support.

**D5. Descending triangle:** lower highs + flat lows; often bearish continuation bias; invalidation on breakout above descending resistance.

**D6. Flags:** sharp impulse (pole) then tight counter-trend channel; continuation bias with pole; invalidation if flag breaks opposite to expected continuation and momentum fails.

**D7. False**

**D8.** Examples: subjective drawing; regime change; low liquidity; news; overcrowded levels; measuring move assumptions; ignoring higher timeframe; forcing patterns onto noise.

# How to turn results into a study plan

1. Pick your **three weakest sections**.
