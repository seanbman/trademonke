Print this or pin it next to your screen. If a box is empty, you do nothing.

## Why checklists work

Beginners do not usually lose because they are stupid. They lose because they improvise under emotion.

A checklist gives you a stable script when your brain wants action. It also gives builders structured data they can compare with platform decisions.

## Before the session

- [ ] Paper / demo / dry-run only  
- [ ] Timer set  
- [ ] Journal open  
- [ ] One market (or tiny list)

## For every idea

### 1) Context

- [ ] Trend up / trend down / chop / unsure (circle one)  
- [ ] Evidence in one line: _______________________  
- [ ] If unsure → **STOP**

### 2) Location

- [ ] Level / zone marked  
- [ ] Why this level (swing / equals / FVG box / other): ________  
- [ ] Optional pattern tag: ________ (or “none”)

### 3) Confirmation

- [ ] Closed candle proof (sweep reclaim / rejection / other): ________  
- [ ] Invalidation level (where the idea is wrong): ________  
- [ ] Target idea (even on paper): ________  

### Risk (paper)

- [ ] Planned size is small and boring  
- [ ] I am not “making it back” from a prior loss  
- [ ] Mood ≥ OK (if not, flat)

## After the idea resolves

- [ ] Screenshot entry theory + outcome  
- [ ] Grade process (A/B/C), not money  
- [ ] One sentence: what I’d teach a friend

## Process grades

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A** | Followed checklist; good or bad outcome doesn’t matter |
| **B** | Minor skip (e.g. vague invalidation) |
| **C** | Broke house rules or skipped a question |

Builders care more about **A-process** frequency than lucky PnL.

## How to use it like a textbook exercise

For one week:

- Fill the checklist for **every** idea  
- Do not hide skipped trades  
- Highlight the line you broke on any C-grade idea

At the end of the week, count:

- How many ideas died at context  
- How many died at location  
- How many died at confirmation  
- How many you still took anyway

That count tells you where your discipline leaks.
