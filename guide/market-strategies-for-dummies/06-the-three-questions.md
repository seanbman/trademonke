## The simple version

Every trade idea must answer three questions **in order**:

1. **Context** — What is the market doing broadly?
2. **Location** — Where is interesting?
3. **Confirmation** — Is something actually defending that idea now?

If any one of those is missing, you do not have a finished idea. You have a hunch.

## Ask in order — do not skip

### 1) Context — What’s the market doing broadly?

Trending up, trending down, or chopping?

If you can’t answer, **stop**. No entry fantasy.

Context is the job of Lesson 5. You are deciding whether your idea is flowing *with* the bigger map or fighting it.

### 2) Location — Where is interesting?

Which level / zone matters *given* that context?

Examples:

- Sweep of prior lows in an uptrend context  
- Edge of a range in chop (advanced — usually skip as a beginner)  
- A gap / imbalance left after a fast move (next lesson)

Location keeps you from taking trades in the middle of nowhere.

### 3) Confirmation — Is something actually defending that idea *now*?

Not “I think it will.” Something you can point to:

- A closed candle reclaiming a level after a wick through  
- A clear rejection after tagging your zone  
- (Later) automated confluence scores from the platform

Confirmation is where patience lives.

## Why this framework is so powerful

It protects you from three common beginner diseases:

| Disease | Which question cures it |
|---|---|
| Random impulse entries | Context |
| Mid-chart boredom trades | Location |
| Jumping in before proof | Confirmation |

## Pocket card

```text
1 CONTEXT:  trend up / trend down / chop / unsure
2 LOCATION: level or zone = ________ @ ________
3 CONFIRM:  what closed-candle proof do I have?
IF any blank → NO TRADE
```

## A full example

Imagine price is broadly trending up.

- **Context:** market still making higher lows  
- **Location:** price returns to an old support zone and tags an FVG box  
- **Confirmation:** a candle wicks below the zone but closes back above it

That is a complete idea. Whether it wins or loses is a different question. But it is **structured**.

## Why builders love this

If every journal row answers these three, they can compare humans vs detectors — and improve the insight tools without guessing what you meant.

## Practice

For the next 10 “almost trades” you notice, fill the pocket card even if you don’t take them. Count how many fail question 1 or 3.

Then review the set and ask:

- Which question do I skip most often?  
- Do I tend to invent confirmation because I am bored?  
- Am I better at context or at location?
