## At a glance

A fair value gap is a **fast-move scar**. Price moved through an area so quickly that traders later watch that area when price comes back.

![FVG / imbalance concepts reference](<../../screenshots/WhatsApp Image 2026-07-10 at 3.50.38 PM.jpeg>)

## Plain definition

A **fair value gap (FVG)** / imbalance is a place where price moved so fast that candles didn’t fully “overlap” in the usual way — leaving a visible gap in the pattern of highs and lows.

Think: the market ran through a hallway without stopping to chat.

## Why people care about gaps

Fast moves often leave behind unfinished business. When price revisits that area later, traders watch to see whether the zone acts like:

- a bounce area  
- a hesitation area  
- a slice-through area

That is why an FVG belongs under **Location** in the three-question model.

## What it is good for

**Location.** Gaps left after a strong move often get revisited later. That revisit is a *candidate* area — not a guaranteed bounce.

## What it is not

- Proof of a conspiracy  
- A reason to ignore context  
- A reason to skip confirmation  
- A promise that price must fill every box immediately

## Beginner recognition drill

On a 5m or 15m chart after a sharp burst:

1. Find three consecutive candles where the middle move left a clear separation between the first and third candle’s ranges.  
2. Draw a box over that separation.  
3. Wait. Journal whether price returned to the box later.

You don’t need the exact platform formula yet. Builders will show you the precise indexing when you’re ready ([master: FVG chapter](../09-fvg-and-order-blocks.md)).

## Tie to the three questions

| Step | FVG role |
|---|---|
| Context | Prefer gaps that agree with the larger direction |
| Location | The gap box *is* a location candidate |
| Confirm | Wait for a closed reaction at/into the box |

## What a good beginner sentence sounds like

> “In an uptrend, price returned to a prior bullish gap near support. I did not enter until a closed candle reacted there.”

That is much better than:

> “Gap touched, buy now.”

## Common beginner mistakes

- Marking every tiny visual separation as an FVG  
- Forcing a trade just because price touched the box  
- Ignoring whether the larger trend agrees  
- Confusing “interesting” with “actionable”

## Practice

Screenshot 3 historical FVGs (by eye). For each:

- Context at the time  
- Did price return?  
- What happened on the first return (bounce, slice through, chop)?
- Was your first reaction too early?

## Builder note

Learner-marked FVGs are valuable comparison data against deterministic `imbalance.fvg` geometry. Repeated mismatch can reveal either poor education or a detector-definition gap.
